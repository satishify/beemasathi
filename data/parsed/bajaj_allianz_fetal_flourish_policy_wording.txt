Annexure I - Description of procedures listed under Fetal Flourish
1. Amniocentesis:
Procedure: A needle is inserted through the mother's abdomen into the amniotic sac to collect amniotic fluid.
Timing: Usually performed from 16 weeks of gestation onwards.
Indications: To detect chromosomal abnormalities (e.g., Down syndrome), genetic disorders, neural tube defects,
and infections.
Inclusions –
Diagnostic testing for chromosomal abnormalities (Down’s, Edwards, Patau), Genetic metabolic disorders in
individuals with a positive family history, Severe structural anomaly detected on ultrasound, Maternal age >35 with
high-risk screening, Recurrent unexplained miscarriages where chromosomal analysis is indicated, Congenital
infection screening (TORCH, CMV).
Exclusions -
• Repeat testing for parental reassurance only.
• Complications management beyond procedure (e.g., maternal infection not covered separately).
• Litigation-related testing (paternity disputes).
• Experimental testing not included in standard guidelines.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
2. Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS):
Procedure: A sample of chorionic villi (tiny projections from the placenta) is taken either through the cervix
(transcervical) or the abdomen (transabdominal).
Timing: Usually performed between 11 to 14 weeks of gestation (from date assigned by ultrasound).
Indications: To diagnose chromosomal abnormalities and genetic disorders earlier in pregnancy compared to
amniocentesis.
Inclusions –
Genetic testing for early detection of chromosomal abnormalities, Indications based on abnormal first-trimester
screen, Confirmatory testing for known parental genetic mutation, Recurrent genetic disorder history in family.
Exclusions –
• Repeat procedures after inadequate sampling without indication.
• Complications such as miscarriage management beyond primary cover.
• Testing outside recommended gestational age window.
• Investigational genetic studies not approved by medical boards.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
3. Percutaneous Umbilical Blood Sampling (PUBS) or Cordocentesis:
Procedure: A needle is inserted through the mother's abdomen into the umbilical cord to obtain fetal blood.
Timing: Usually performed after 18 weeks of gestation.
Indications: To diagnose and treat fetal blood conditions, infections, and certain genetic disorders; also used for
fetal blood transfusions.
Inclusions –
Diagnosis of fetal anemia, Intrauterine infection confirmation, Genetic and metabolic testing where
amniocentesis/CVS is inconclusive, Guidance for intrauterine transfusion, Evaluation of platelet alloimmunization.
Exclusions -
• Non-standard investigational uses.
• Repeated cordocentesis for poor sampling unless indicated.
• Complications requiring neonatal intensive care beyond prenatal cover.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
4. Fetal Tissue Biopsy:
Procedure: A sample of fetal tissue is obtained using a needle inserted through the mother's abdomen.
Timing: Varies depending on the condition being investigated.

5. Amnioinfusion:
Procedure: Saline or another fluid is infused into the amniotic sac via a needle through the abdomen.
Timing: Performed during pregnancy when there is a need to improve ultrasound imaging of the fetus.
Indications: To treat conditions like oligohydramnios (low amniotic fluid) and to improve ultrasound imaging of the
fetus.
Inclusions –
Severe oligohydramnios with fetal compromise, Meconium dilution to prevent aspiration
Exclusions –
• Repeated infusion for borderline cases without fetal distress.
• Associated maternal complications beyond immediate procedure.
• Non-standard solutions/experimental infusions.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
6. Amnio-reduction
Procedure: Removal of excess amniotic fluid using a needle inserted into the amniotic sac under ultrasound
guidance.
Timing: Usually performed in the second trimester, especially in cases of polyhydramnios or twin-to-twin
transfusion syndrome (TTTS).
Indications: To relieve maternal symptoms, reduce uterine overdistension, and improve placental perfusion or
manage TTTS.
Inclusions –
Twin-to-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS) with severe polyhydramnios, Polyhydramnios causing maternal
respiratory compromise, Symptomatic relief in maternal distress, Ultrasound-guided therapeutic drainage.
Exclusions -
• Repeated procedures beyond guideline thresholds.
• Out-of-protocol interventions not supported by evidence.
• Experimental use outside TTTS/polyhydramnios.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
7. Thoracoamniotic Shunt for Fetal Pleural Effusion
Condition: Accumulation of fluid in the fetal chest cavity (pleural effusion) can compress the lungs and heart.
Procedure: A shunt is placed to drain the fluid from the chest cavity into the amniotic sac.
Timing: Varies depending on the severity and progression of the effusion.
Benefits: Relieves pressure on the lungs and heart, improving fetal development and survival.
Inclusions –
Severe fetal pleural effusion with lung compression, Hydrops due to thoracic fluid accumulation.
Exclusions -
• Procedures without documented compromise.
• Replacements due to displacement unless medically justified.
• Non-standard devices.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.

8. Fetoscopy:
Procedure: A small camera (fetoscope) is inserted into the uterus through a small incision in the abdomen.
Timing: Typically performed after 18 weeks of gestation.
Inclusions –
Direct visualization for targeted fetal interventions, Entry for therapeutic procedures (laser, biopsy)
Exclusions –
• Experimental visualizations.
• Repeated procedures without indication.
• Complications unrelated to primary indication.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
9. Fetoscopic Laser Surgery for Twin-to-Twin Transfusion Syndrome (TTTS):
Condition: TTTS is a condition that affects identical twins sharing a placenta where blood flow between the twins
is imbalanced.
Procedure: Using a fetoscope, a laser is used to coagulate the abnormal blood vessels connecting the twins'
circulations.
Timing: Typically performed between 16 and 26 weeks of gestation.
Benefits: Improves outcomes by balancing blood flow and reducing the risk of heart failure and other
complications.
Inclusions –
Monochorionic twin pregnancies with TTTS, Laser coagulation of placental anastomoses / chorioangiomas
Exclusions -
• Dichorionic pregnancies.
• Repeat procedures outside guideline-based recurrence.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
10. Fetal Reduction
a. First Trimester Reduction
Procedure: Injection of potassium chloride into the heart of one or more fetuses under ultrasound guidance to
reduce the number of fetuses in a multi-Fetal pregnancy.
Timing: Typically done between 11-14 weeks of gestation.
Indications: To reduce risks associated with high-order multiple pregnancies, including preterm birth, low birth
weight, and maternal complications.
Inclusions –
High-order multiple pregnancies (≥3 fetuses), Maternal/fetal compromise due to crowding, Selective reduction in
anomalous fetuses. (Genetic + Structural).
Exclusions
• Elective reduction in uncomplicated twins.
• Procedures beyond recommended gestation window.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
b. Selective Fetal Reduction (SFR) / Second Trimester Reduction
Procedure: SFR must be performed using a Vaso-occlusive technique only: Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA) or Bipolar
Cord Occlusion (BCO) of the affected twin's umbilical cord.
Timing: The procedure is covered when performed during the second trimester, typically between 16 and 27 weeks
of gestation, determined by the treating Fetal Medicine specialist.
Benefits: The primary goal is to improve the survival and reduce the risk of severe neurological injury to the
remaining healthy co-twin, and to increase the gestational age at delivery.

13. Fetoscopic Endotracheal Occlusion (FETO) for Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia (CDH):
Condition: CDH is a defect where abdominal organs herniate into the chest cavity, affecting lung development
Procedure: A balloon is placed in the fetal trachea to promote lung growth by blocking fluid from escaping the
lungs. A second procedure is required later to remove the balloon.
Timing: Occlusion is typically performed between 27+0 and 29+6 weeks of gestation for severe cases. Balloon
removal is performed in a second procedure, usually between 32 and 34 weeks of gestation, to allow for
maturation before birth.
Benefits: Enhances lung development, improving survival rates and respiratory outcomes after birth.
Inclusions –
Severe congenital diaphragmatic hernia with poor prognosis, Fetoscopic balloon tracheal occlusion.
Exclusions -
• Cases with non-isolated CDH (major co-existing structural or chromosomal anomalies).
• Multiple gestations.
• Maternal contraindications (e.g., severe obesity, placenta previa).
• Short cervix (≤ 15mm).

14. Amniotic Band Syndrome (ABS) Surgery:
Condition: ABS occurs when fibrous bands in the amniotic sac constrict fetal parts, leading to deformities or
amputations.
Procedure: Using a fetoscope, the constricting bands are cut to release the affected fetal parts.
Timing: Varies depending on the severity and location of the bands.
Benefits: Prevents further damage and preserves the function and development of the affected parts.
Inclusions –
Documented limb constriction risk, Fetal survival/end-organ compromise due to bands, Fetoscopic release
procedures
Exclusions –
• Mild bands without compromise.
• Cosmetic-only correction.
• Repeated attempts with low success likelihood.
• Experimental techniques.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
15. Fetal Aortic Valvuloplasty for Severe Aortic Stenosis:
Condition: Severe aortic stenosis can lead to underdevelopment of the left ventricle (hypoplastic left heart
syndrome).
Procedure: A balloon is inserted into the fetal heart to open the narrowed aortic valve.
Timing: Usually performed between 20 and 30 weeks of gestation.
Benefits: Improves blood flow through the heart, promoting normal development of the left ventricle.
Inclusions –
Severe aortic stenosis with evolving hypoplastic left heart syndrome, Ultrasound-guided intrauterine balloon
dilation.
Exclusions –
• Mild/moderate stenosis.
• Repeat dilations without progression.
• Procedures outside recommended gestation.
• Experimental catheter techniques.
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
16. Intra-uterine transfusion (IUT)
Procedure: Transfusion of compatible red blood cells directly into the Fetal umbilical vein or peritoneal cavity
under ultrasound guidance.
Timing: Typically initiated after 18 weeks of gestation and may be repeated every 1–3 weeks until term.
Inclusions –
Severe fetal anemia (MCA PSV MoM > 1.5) (Rh isoimmunization, parvovirus), Platelet transfusion in alloimmune
thrombocytopenia, Cordocentesis-guided transfusion, Blood product preparation, cross-matching, and procedure,
Multiple transfusions as medically required (upto 2).
Exclusions -
• Elective transfusions for borderline anemia. (MCA PSV MoM < 1.5).
• Requests without documented cause.
• Use of untested/unapproved donor sources.
• Complications requiring NICU beyond fetal procedure cover
• Procedures without prior screening justification.
